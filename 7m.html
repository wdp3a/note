Q.1

How do you look at your basic aspirations? Is it 'To Be', 'To Get', 'To Become'? Justify with examples.

Basic Human Aspirations

Every human being has two fundamental aspirations:

  • Happiness (Sukh) — a state of harmony within the Self (I)
  • Prosperity (Samridhi) — having more than enough physical facilities

Three Ways of Looking at Aspirations

'To Get' Accumulate material things — money, status, fame. Physical/external focus.
'To Become' Achieve a position — doctor, engineer, CEO. Identity-based focus.
'To Be' Attain a state of inner harmony — to be happy, to be just. Internal/value focus.

Which is the Right Priority?

The right priority is 'To Be' — i.e., to ensure right understanding and values within the Self. This is because:

  • 'To Get' gives only temporary pleasure. A person who gets a car is happy for a few days but wants a bigger car later — this is endless.
  • 'To Become' makes us dependent on external approval and positions which can be taken away.
  • 'To Be' means having a stable, harmonious inner state that does not depend on external conditions.

Justification with Examples

AspirationExampleResult
To GetEarning more money, buying bigger houseTemporary satisfaction, increasing wants
To BecomeBecoming a manager or a celebrityDependent on others' approval, fear of losing status
To BeBeing honest, being caring in all situationsStable happiness, right conduct regardless of circumstances

A student who studies to be knowledgeable and competent (To Be) gains lasting wisdom, whereas one who studies to get marks (To Get) may cheat and still remain ignorant.

Concept Booster

Think of it as a foundation: "To Be" is the inner foundation (values/happiness), while "To Get" and "To Become" are outer constructions. Without a strong foundation, the outer structure collapses. Right priority = build the inside first.

Q.2

Explain the difference between needs of Self (I) and Body. Can they be fulfilled interchangeably? Verify on yourself.

The Human Being: I + Body

A human being is a co-existence of Self (I) — the conscious unit, and Body — the material/physical unit. Both have distinct needs.

Needs of the Self (I)

  • Happiness (Sukh), Peace (Shanti), Satisfaction (Santosh), Bliss (Ananda)
  • These are continuous and do not diminish over time
  • Fulfilled by: right understanding, right feelings, right relationships
  • These are qualitative needs — not measured in quantity

Needs of the Body

  • Food, shelter, clothing, rest, physical comfort
  • These are limited and can be saturated (you can eat only so much)
  • Fulfilled by: physical facilities (material things)
  • These are quantitative needs — can be measured

Q.4

Harmony in 'I' means understanding characteristics and activities of 'I'. Explain.

What is Harmony in 'I'?

Harmony in the Self (I) refers to a state where all the activities of the Self are aligned with right understanding. There is no internal conflict, contradiction, or confusion.

Characteristics of the Self (I)

  • Desire (Ichha): The Self generates desires — what do I want?
  • Thought (Vichar): The Self analyses and thinks about how to achieve it
  • Expectation (Asha): The Self expects certain outcomes

These three form the inner faculties of the Self.

Four Activities of the Self (I)

Knowing (Jnana) The Self understands things — right or wrong knowledge
Assuming (Manya) The Self assumes / believes without full knowing
Recognizing (Pahachana) Identifying relationships and values
Fulfilling (Nibhana) Acting on what is known/assumed

When is There Harmony?

  • Harmony = when Knowing, Assuming, Recognizing, and Fulfilling are all aligned with right understanding
  • When we assume without right knowing, or when our feelings don't match our values, there is disharmony
Example of Disharmony: A person knows honesty is right (Knowing) but assumes that lying helps them succeed (Assuming) → conflict → disharmony in Self → anxiety and guilt.

Example of Harmony: A person knows and believes that honesty is right AND acts honestly → peace and integrity.

Result of Harmony in I

  • Inner peace and contentment
  • Natural, effortless ethical conduct
  • No internal contradiction between thoughts, feelings, and actions
Concept Booster

Harmony in I is like all instruments of an orchestra playing in sync. If one instrument plays out of tune (e.g., our desires don't match our values), the whole music (our life) becomes discordant. Right understanding tunes all four activities together.

Distinguish between happiness and excitement. Critically examine how sensations cannot be the source of continuous happiness.

Why Sensations Cannot Provide Continuous Happiness

Sensations are inputs received by the body through the five sense organs (sight, sound, taste, touch, smell). These are experienced by the Body — not the Self.

  • Adaptation Effect: Any repeated sensation loses intensity. The first bite of chocolate is exciting; the 50th bite is not. This shows sensations are self-exhausting.
  • Craving cycle: Sensations create a craving-fulfillment loop — once the sensation ends, the craving returns (often stronger). This is a cycle of dependence, not happiness.
  • Diminishing returns: More and more is needed to achieve the same pleasure — this leads to addiction (drugs, screen time, junk food).
  • Body ≠ Self: Since the Self (I) is not the body, satisfying the body's sensory inputs cannot satisfy the Self's need for happiness.
Critical Examination: A person who eats their favorite food when feeling sad does not become genuinely happy — the sadness returns after the eating stops. A wealthy person surrounded by luxuries can still be deeply unhappy. This proves sensations address only the Body, not the Self.
Concept Booster

Sensations are like scratching an itch — they provide temporary relief but the itch (craving) returns, often stronger. True happiness is like curing the underlying skin condition — permanent resolution. Sensations treat the symptom; right understanding treats the cause.

Q.9

Explain the feelings of Care and Guidance. What role do these feelings have in parent-child relationships?

The Feeling of Care (Mamata / Sneh)

Care means to feel responsible for the well-being and growth of another person. It involves:

  • Wishing for the happiness and proper development of the other
  • Taking active steps to nurture their physical, emotional, and intellectual growth
  • Care is unconditional — it doesn't expect anything in return
  • It ensures that the basic physical needs of the other person are fulfilled with affection

The Feeling of Guidance (Sneh-poorn Margdarshan)

Guidance means to help another person understand what is right, helping them develop right understanding and correct values. It involves:

  • Sharing wisdom and knowledge lovingly, not forcefully
  • Helping the other see the consequences of their choices
  • Encouraging self-exploration — not just giving orders
  • It is value-based mentoring, not mere instruction

Role in Parent-Child Relationship

FeelingRole of ParentBenefit for Child
CareEnsures nutritious food, health, safe environment; emotional warmthPhysical health, emotional security, trust
GuidanceTeaches values, explains right and wrong, encourages self-reflectionRight understanding, good character, independence

Without Care, the child lacks physical and emotional security. Without Guidance, the child lacks direction and values. Together, they make parents the child's first and most important value educators.

Important Distinction: Care without Guidance can lead to overprotection and spoiling. Guidance without Care can be cold and authoritarian. A caring guide is the ideal — like a gardener who both waters the plant (care) and provides a supportive structure (guidance).
Concept Booster

Care = "I want you to be well." Guidance = "I want you to understand what is right." A parent who only provides food and things (care) but never teaches values raises a well-fed but confused adult. A parent who lectures without warmth (guidance without care) raises a resentful adult. Both together = holistic parenting.

Meaning of Respect (Samman)

Respect means right evaluation — evaluating a person for what they truly are. Since every human being has the same Self (I), every person deserves to be recognized as a conscious, valuable human being.

  • Respect is NOT flattery, fear, or subservience
  • It means acknowledging the human potential and inherent dignity in every person
  • True respect is continuous and unconditional

How We Make Wrong Differentiation in Respect

Currently, we evaluate people based on physical or material attributes rather than their humanity:

Wealth Rich person = more respect; poor person = less respect
Position Senior officer respected, junior worker neglected
Caste/Religion Upper caste treated with more dignity
Physical Appearance Attractive people treated more favorably

This is wrong evaluation — we are confusing the instrument (body/position/wealth) with the human being (Self/I).

Minimum Content of Respect for a Human Being

The minimum that every human being deserves:

  • Recognition as a human being: Every person has the same Self (I) — conscious, capable of happiness and suffering
  • Acknowledgment of their desire for happiness: Every person wants happiness — this is universally true
  • Right evaluation of their qualities: See what they are — not just their wealth, caste, or position
  • Non-humiliation: Not insulting or belittling anyone on the basis of physical/material attributes
Key Insight: Respect does not mean treating everyone identically in every situation — but it means treating every person with the recognition of their inherent human dignity. A cleaner and a CEO are both equally human and deserve this basic recognition.

Q.11

What is justice? How does it lead to mutual happiness?

Meaning of Justice (Nyaya)

Justice in UHV means the recognition, fulfillment, and evaluation of relationships in a harmonious manner. It is not merely a legal concept but a deeply felt human value.

Justice involves three steps:

  1. Recognition (Pahachana): Correctly identifying the relationship and its values
  2. Fulfillment (Nibhana): Fulfilling the expected feelings in the relationship
  3. Evaluation (Mulyankan): Assessing whether the relationship is being fulfilled rightly

Justice in Relationships

  • In every human relationship, both parties expect certain values (trust, respect, care, love)
  • Justice = when both parties correctly identify and fulfil these values
  • Injustice = when one or both parties fail to recognize or act on these values

When one lane (one person) blocks or ignores the other, there's an accident (injustice). When both flow well — mutual happiness.

Human-to-human relationship is inherent — not created by external factors like culture, laws, or social contracts. This is because:

Love in UHV means the feeling of being related to every human being — a sense of belonging and oneness. It is:

Love as the Highest Relational Value

When we feel love for all human beings, it means we:

Q.13

Comprehensive Human Goal — Overview

The comprehensive human goal operates at all four levels: Individual, Family, Society, and Nature. Two critical dimensions for achieving this are:

Education refers to the development of right understanding — not just information transfer but the development of a correctly seeing human being.

  • Sanskar is received from family, teachers, environment, and society

Health here means both physical well-being and the right understanding of the body's needs — knowing what truly keeps the body healthy (nutritious food, rest, natural living).

  • Sanyam prevents overindulgence in food, sleep, sensory pleasures
  • It ensures the body remains a healthy instrument for a long time
  • It also means proper use of physical facilities — not wastage or exploitation

Need for Value Education

  • Rising rates of corruption, violence, depression, and family breakdown show a value deficit

Society Level Justice, fearlessness, mutual fulfillment

Q.15

What is ethical human conduct? Explain in terms of values, policies and character with appropriate examples.

Ethical Human Conduct

Ethical human conduct means acting in ways that are mutually fulfilling for oneself and others, based on right understanding. It involves consistency between inner values, outer policies/actions, and the character one develops over time.

1. Values (Mulya)

Values are universal inner standards of right and wrong — they are the same for all human beings:

  • Trust, Respect, Affection, Care, Guidance, Gratitude, Reverence, Love
  • Example: Honesty — the value of being truthful is universally accepted as right
  • Values guide what should be done, regardless of circumstance

2. Policies (Niti)

Policies are context-based guidelines derived from values — they tell us how to act in specific situations:

  • A doctor's policy: "I will not share a patient's confidential information" (derived from the value of trust)
  • An engineer's policy: "I will not approve a structurally unsafe design even under pressure" (from value of responsibility)
  • Policies can change with context, but values they are based on remain constant

3. Character (Charitra)

Character is the consistent manifestation of values and policies over time — it is what a person is, not just what they do occasionally:

  • A person of character acts honestly not because they are watched, but because it is natural to them
  • Character is built by repeatedly aligning actions with values

Q.17

Explain how excellence is different from 'to be special' and how it leads toward a harmonious society.

'To Be Special' — The Problem

'To be special' means wanting to be better than others, to stand out, to be recognized as superior. This is:

  • Comparative and competitive — requires others to be 'less'
  • Based on ego and external validation
  • Creates insecurity, jealousy, and constant anxiety (fear of losing specialness)
  • Fundamentally divisive — separates "special" from "ordinary"

Excellence — The Right Understanding

Excellence means performing one's role and responsibilities to the fullest extent — not to be better than someone else, but because it is the right way of being.

  • Climate change: Disrupting the carbon cycle through excess fossil fuel burning
  • Biodiversity loss: Destroying species and ecosystems — breaking the web of mutual fulfillment
  • Linear economy: Human production follows a take-make-dispose model, unlike nature's circular model
Root Cause: This disruption stems from humans operating with animal or deluded consciousness — "What can I extract?" instead of "How can I co-exist and contribute?"

Q.36

Explain the meaning of profession and explore the need of ethical competence in profession.

Meaning of Profession

A profession is not merely a job — it is a role in society through which one contributes to the well-being of others while also sustaining oneself. Key aspects:

  • It involves specialized knowledge and skills acquired through education and training
  • It is a role of responsibility — the professional's actions affect the lives of others
  • Examples: Engineering, medicine, law, teaching, architecture, management

Ethical Competence in Profession

Ethical competence is the ability to act in one's professional role in ways that are aligned with core human values — honesty, responsibility, care, justice — even under pressure to compromise.

Why Ethical Competence is Needed

  • Professional decisions affect many: An engineer's design affects thousands of users; a doctor's prescription affects the patient's health. Wrong decisions cause harm.
  • Trust is the foundation: Society trusts professionals because of their expertise AND their integrity. Without ethical competence, this trust breaks down.
  • Power asymmetry: Professionals have more knowledge than their clients — this creates potential for exploitation. Ethics prevents misuse of this power advantage.
  • Long-term consequences: Many professional decisions have long-term impacts (environmental decisions, public health policies). Ethics ensures these are considered.
  • Systemic health: Professions shape society. Ethical professionals collectively build a just, functional society.
Key Formula: Technical Competence + Ethical Competence = True Professional Excellence
Concept Booster

A surgeon with perfect technical skill but poor ethics might perform unnecessary operations for money. A surgeon with good ethics but poor technical skill might harm patients unintentionally. Society needs surgeons (and all professionals) who are both technically excellent AND ethically grounded. One without the other is dangerous.

Activities of Imagination in the Self (I)

The Self continuously engages in imagination — it is the primary activity of I. The three forms of imagination are:

  • Desire (Ichha): Generating wants and aspirations — "I want peace", "I want recognition"
  • Thought (Vichar): Deliberating and planning — "How should I act to achieve this?"
  • Expectation (Asha): Anticipating outcomes — "If I do X, I will get Y"

These three form a continuous mental activity — the "inner chatter" of the Self.

How Imagination Gets Enslaved Without Right Understanding

Without right understanding, the imagination becomes enslaved to sensory inputs and conditioned beliefs:

  • Desire becomes craving: Instead of desiring genuine happiness, we start desiring sensory pleasures (more food, more money, more likes on social media) because we wrongly believe these will give happiness
  • Thought becomes manipulation: Planning not on values but on how to get more for oneself — deceiving others, cutting corners
  • Expectation becomes anxiety: When expectations are tied to external outcomes (wealth, status), they generate constant fear of not achieving or losing them
Result of Enslaved Imagination: The Self becomes trapped in an endless loop of craving → partial satisfaction → more craving. This is the root of addiction, anxiety, depression, and restlessness.

How Right Understanding Frees Imagination

  • Desire aligns with genuine happiness (values, harmony, contribution)
  • Thought becomes constructive and ethical planning
  • Expectation becomes realistic and founded on inner states, not external outcomes
Concept Booster

Without right understanding, imagination is like a powerful horse with no rider — it runs wherever instinct and habit take it (usually toward sensory pleasures and ego). Right understanding is the skilled rider who guides the horse toward meaningful destinations. The horse's power (imagination) is not wasted — it's directed rightly.

Q.40

Level 3: Developmental Respect People with higher competence are given acknowledgment for that — but this acknowledgment does not diminish those with lesser competence.

Q.41

Explain the submergence of nature in space. How does this relate to a student going through a professional course?

Submergence of Nature in Space

Submergence (Vyaptata) means that all units of nature — every atom, every living being, every element — exist within space, surrounded by space, and cannot exist outside of it.

  • Space is the universal container — all-pervading, continuous, and undivided
  • Every material unit has its own boundary but is submerged in the continuous space around it
  • No unit is isolated — every unit is connected to all others through the common medium of space
  • This submergence implies fundamental interconnectedness — no unit exists in isolation

This is the basis for co-existence — all units are submerged in the same space, making them inherently related.

Relationship to a Student in a Professional Course

The metaphor of submergence applies powerfully to a student's situation:

  • You are not isolated: Just as every unit is submerged in space (the larger whole), a student exists within and is supported by family, society, and nature — not as an independent atom
  • Interdependence with all: The knowledge you acquire, the resources you use (electricity, food, internet) — all come from the larger social and natural system you are submerged in
  • Responsibility flows from submergence: Since you are part of the larger whole, your actions (as a future professional) affect the whole. You cannot say "it's not my problem"
  • Contribution to the whole: Your professional skills should enrich the system you are submerged in — just as every natural unit contributes to the larger order
Concept Booster

A fish in the ocean is "submerged" — the ocean sustains it, and the fish's health affects the ocean's health. Similarly, a student is submerged in society — society created the educational system, the infrastructure, the knowledge base you're using. Recognizing this submergence generates responsibility: "I must contribute back to what sustains me."

Right Evaluation (Samyak Mulyankan)

Right evaluation means evaluating a person, object, or situation for what it truly is — based on its actual nature, qualities, and purpose.

  • Seeing a knife as a kitchen tool → right evaluation
  • Seeing a person as a conscious human being who deserves dignity → right evaluation
  • Evaluating a professional by their competence, ethics, and contribution → right evaluation

Wrong evaluation means attributing wrong properties to something — either undervaluing or overvaluing based on irrelevant criteria.

  • Thinking money can buy happiness (overvaluing money's capability) → wrong evaluation
  • Evaluating a person by their caste, wealth, or appearance → wrong evaluation of human worth
  • Believing that only physically attractive people are "successful" → wrong evaluation
